Finnish for Practical Nurse (Lähihoitajan suomi) — A complete spoken-Finnish course for lähihoitaja work in elderly care — from first hello to shift handover.
Beginner (A1) · 11 chapters · 52 lessons — Everyday spoken Finnish for the care floor: kind, simple phrases you can use from your very first shift.
1 · First Words (Ensikohtaaminen) — Your very first exchanges on the floor — names, greetings, and the words that buy you time as a beginner.
* "Hi, I'm your nurse today" — Introducing yourself simply and saying why you're there, in everyday spoken Finnish.
* Catching a name — Asking someone's name, repeating it back, and learning how they like to be called.
* "Slowly, please" — The lifeline phrases for when Finnish comes too fast: say it again, slower.
* Yes, no, not sure — Answering and reading simple yes/no — and admitting honestly when you don't understand.
* Numbers out loud — Room numbers, times, and counts said and heard clearly out loud.
2 · Asking & Reassuring (Lupa ja luottamus) — Asking permission before you touch or help, and the small words that build trust in care.
* "May I?" — Asking permission before helping, touching, or entering — always first, always kindly.
* "I'll tell you what's next" — Narrating the next step so nothing you do comes as a surprise.
* Te or sä? — Choosing polite te or familiar sä, and why it matters with older people.
* Small reassurances — "It's okay", "I'm right here", "almost done" — the small words that calm.
3 · The Shift Begins (Vuoro alkaa) — Arriving for your shift and starting the morning round — waking, greeting, and easing into the day.
* "Good morning, did you sleep well?" — Gentle wake-ups and the first warm words of the day in spoken Finnish.
* "Shall we get up together?" — Offering a hand and inviting someone to rise at their own pace.
* "What would you like to wear today?" — Giving real choices while helping with dressing — small decisions, real dignity.
* A new day: light, date, weather — Opening the curtains and orienting someone to the day, date, and weather.
* "Here are your morning pills" — Helping with morning medication: naming it, water, and a calm check it's gone down.
4 · Washing & Dressing (Pesut ja pukeminen) — Personal care with dignity — washing, dressing, and the bathroom, narrated gently step by step.
* "Let's freshen up" — Inviting someone to wash, offering warm water, and going at their pace.
* Step by step, getting dressed — Naming clothes and guiding each arm and leg without rushing.
* The bathroom, with dignity — Calm, private words for toileting help that protect self-respect.
* "Tell me if it's too cold" — Checking water, comfort, and consent all the way through.
* Clean and comfortable — Finishing up: drying off, fresh clothes, and "there, all done".
5 · At the Table (Ruokapöydässä) — Mealtimes as the heart of the day — serving, encouraging, and sharing food and coffee.
* "Are you hungry?" — Asking about appetite and offering the meal in simple, inviting words.
* A little more? — Encouraging eating gently, and reading when someone has had enough.
* "What do you like?" — Learning food likes and dislikes so meals feel personal, not routine.
* Coffee, the Finnish way — The kahvi ritual: offering, pouring, and the small talk that comes with it.
6 · Moving Together (Liikkeelle yhdessä) — Helping someone stand, walk, and transfer safely with clear cues that keep you both steady.
* "Lean on me" — Offering your arm and building trust before you help someone move.
* Up on three — Counting and cueing a stand-up so you rise together, safely.
* One step at a time — Walking together with a frame or your support, encouraging each step.
* Into the chair — Guiding a safe sit-down and transfer to a chair or wheelchair.
* "Careful, slowly" — The little safety words for wet floors, thresholds, and tired legs.
7 · Care at Home (Kotihoito) — Care in someone's own home — arriving, checking in, doing the visit, and leaving, where you're a guest not the host.
* At the door, keys and all — Knocking, the key safe, and announcing yourself before you step inside.
* "How have you been?" — The quick wellbeing check that opens a visit: sleep, eating, mood, the night alone.
* What needs doing today — Agreeing the visit's tasks together: meds, a meal, a little tidying.
* A guest in their home — Respecting their space, their rules, and their own way of doing things.
* "I'll be back tomorrow" — Wrapping up, locking up, and saying clearly when you'll next come.
8 · Listening to the Body (Kehon kieli) — The body in plain words and how to ask what hurts — the start of noticing and reporting changes.
* The body in plain words — Everyday names for the body parts you'll point to and ask about daily.
* "Where does it hurt?" — Asking where the trouble is and following a pointing finger.
* Ache, sharp, or dizzy — Telling apart kinds of pain and that woozy, dizzy feeling.
* Hot, cold, tired, thirsty — Reading and asking about the basic body states all day long.
* "Since when?" — Asking when something started, so you can pass it on to a nurse.
9 · When Memory Fades (Kun muisti pettää) — Talking with residents living with memory loss — calm words, gentle redirection, meeting them where they are.
* Saying your name again — Reintroducing yourself warmly each time, never as a correction.
* "You're safe here" — Reassuring words when someone feels lost, anxious, or far from home.
* Going along with it — Stepping kindly into their reality instead of arguing with confusion.
* Remembering out loud — Reminiscing: asking about the past to spark calm, happy talk.
* A gentle change of subject — Redirecting softly when someone is upset or stuck on a worry.
10 · Family at the Door (Omaiset paikalla) — Meeting residents' families warmly — welcoming visitors, sharing the day, and keeping kind limits.
* "You must be...?" — Greeting a visitor and placing who they are to the resident.
* How the day went — Sharing a simple, honest update on mood, eating, and sleep.
* "I'll pass that on" — Taking a message or request and promising it reaches the team.
* Kindly, not now — Gentle phrases that protect rest, mealtimes, and privacy.
11 · Passing It On (Raportti ja kirjaaminen) — Handing over your shift and recording care — telling the team what changed, in speech and simple notes.
* The handover, briefly — Telling the next shift the essentials: who, what changed, what to watch.
* "Something's different today" — Flagging a change in a resident clearly to a nurse or colleague.
* Writing it down — The simplest care-record words: ate, slept, mood, pain.
* "Can you help me with...?" — Asking a colleague for a hand or a second pair of eyes.
